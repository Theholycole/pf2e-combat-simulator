# Spellcasting: decisions

Milestone 2, step 4: creatures cast spells in the simulation through the resolution core (`src/engine/spells.js`). The core's `save` family resolves saves and spell attacks into changes, and the engine applies the ones it models. Where PF2e has no single answer for an abstract, gridless fight, each question below has a default, so the engine can run. **The user confirmed every default (S1–S21) on 2026-10-08, and chose S22 on 2026-10-14.** Tune the weights if results look off.

| # | Question | Default chosen | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | Which spells does a creature cast? | Any non-ritual spell whose plan the core can resolve (kinds `save`, `healing`, `direct-damage` and `effect` so far) and that has creature targets, an area or (for effects) the caster itself. Everything else stays in the gap list. Note-only spells (`spell-note`, `message`: Detect Magic, Translocate and the like) are listed as **utility spells**, which don't lower the reliability note. | The gap list stays honest. Utility spells are on most monsters but rarely change a fight. |
| S2 | How many times? | Prepared: once per slot, not already expended. Spontaneous: the rank's remaining slots, shared by its spells. Innate: its uses, or at will when it has none listed. Focus spells: the focus pool. Cantrips: at will. A full simulation uses the creature as it is now; a fresh one starts with everything unspent. | PF2e's own sheet data. |
| S3 | At what rank? | A prepared or spontaneous spell at its slot's rank. Cantrips and focus spells at half the creature's level, rounded up. Signature spells aren't cast at higher ranks. | That's PF2e's rule for cantrips and focus spells. Heightening signature spells needs a choice of rank, and has its own question for later. |
| S4 | Spells that take "1 to 3" actions. | Two actions. Reactions, free actions and spells cast in a minute or more aren't cast. | Two is the usual choice. Heal and Harm's variable versions arrive with the healing family. |
| S5 | What does an area catch, with no grid? | Everyone in one Melee (both sides), or enemies standing apart in one band. A **capacity** limits it, enemies first: bursts and emanations 5 ft → 2, 10 → 4, 15 → 6, 20 → 8, 30+ → 10; cones 15 → 3, 30 → 5, 60 → 8; lines 30 → 3, 60 → 4, 120 → 6. Loose creatures count half. A burst into the caster's own Melee catches the caster too; a cone or line doesn't. An emanation with no range covers the caster's Melee. | A Melee is a cluster of adjacent creatures, and bands are 30 feet apart. The capacity is roughly how many creatures fit in the template. |
| S6 | Friendly fire. | Allies caught in an area count against the cast, at their own threat value. Creatures choose freely, and will hit allies when it's worth it. | Lets intelligent and reckless foes look different without a separate setting. |
| S7 | How does a creature value a spell? | Damage as a share of the target's HP times its threat, like a Strike (persistent damage counts twice). Conditions as rounds of the target's threat: stunned N = N/3; slowed N = N/3 a round for up to 3 rounds (×0.6); fleeing 0.8 a round; paralyzed 1 a round; asleep 0.7 a round; restrained 0.7; blinded 0.4; grabbed or immobilized 0.2; dazzled 0.15; prone 0.15; off-guard 0.1 a round; frightened, sickened, clumsy, enfeebled, drained, stupefied and fatigued by their value. A death effect is worth the target's remaining HP. | The same units as Strikes, so the planner can weigh a spell against attacking. The condition weights are estimates; tune them as results come in. |
| S8 | Decisions the GM would answer ("is it made of liquid?", Murmuration's damage type). | Each takes its default: no adjustment, and the first listed option. | The simulator has no GM. A creature's traits still apply automatically (Parch on a water creature). |
| S9 | Durations. | "For N rounds" ends at the start of the caster's turn N rounds later. Minutes and longer last the whole fight. A condition with no duration follows its own rules: frightened goes down by 1 at the end of each of the creature's turns, and stunned is used up. | PF2e's duration rule. |
| S10 | Sleep and other spells' unconsciousness. | **Asleep**: can't act, off-guard, and stays in the fight; any damage wakes it. | A sleeping creature isn't down: it can't die from dying or be captured. |
| S11 | Fleeing from a spell (Fear). | The creature spends its turn moving away. When the duration ends it returns to the fight; only morale's fleeing makes a creature leave. | A spell's fear wears off. A rout doesn't. |
| S12 | Spell attacks and Strikes. | Spell attacks count toward the multiple attack penalty, and casting in a Melee provokes Attacks of Opportunity (manipulate). | PF2e's attack and manipulate traits. |
| S13 | Blinded, dazzled and stupefied casters. | Blinded: a DC 11 flat check per target of a targeted spell or Strike. Dazzled: DC 5. Stupefied N: a DC 5 + N flat check to cast, or the spell (and its slot) is lost. | PF2e's rules for hidden and concealed targets, and for stupefied. |
| S14 | Follow-up saves (Phantasmal Calamity). | Resolved straight away, on the same creature. | The card's follow-up button, without the wait. |
| S15 | Heal and Harm. | Their plans are basic saves for vitality or void damage. PF2e's rule applies (vitality harms only creatures with void healing, void only those without), so a Heal does nothing to living foes. Once the spell compiler writes the healing payload, recipients (living for Heal, undead for Harm) are healed and don't save. Until then the healing half is a gap. | Found in the bestiary check: before the rule, a monster's Heal damaged living PCs. |
| S16 | How is healing valued? | The ally's threat × the share of its maximum HP restored (no more than it's missing), × an urgency of 2.5 × (share of HP lost)^1.5 (about 0.2 at 82% HP, 0.9 at half, 1.6 at a quarter). A downed PC counts ×3 at its side's average threat. Healing a foe counts against the cast. A downed PC who is healed wakes, with dying removed, wounded +1 and still prone, and rejoins the near band. | Healing is worth most to the ally closest to dropping, and bringing back a downed PC is worth most of all. Waking on healing is PF2e's rule. |
| S18 | Buffs and debuffs (PF2e spell effects: Heroism, Protection, Haste, Mystic Armor, Leaden Steps). | The effect item's rules are read:<br>• **Modelled:** flat modifiers to AC, saves, attack rolls, damage and spell DCs and attacks; temporary HP; fast healing; resistances, weaknesses and immunities; granted conditions (Haste → quickened); unconditional extra damage dice, at their average.<br>• **Skipped as situational:** rules with predicates other than the effect's level (Courageous Anthem's bonus against fear), and selectors chosen at cast time.<br>• **Gaps:** battle forms, new Strikes, rerolls and the like.<br>• Bonuses stack by PF2e's rules (the best of each type, untyped ones add), and status penalties join the conditions'.<br>• An effect lasts its duration (minutes ×10 rounds, longer for the whole fight) from the caster's turn.<br>• The planner aims it at allies (or the caster, for self-only spells) and values it in rounds of the ally's threat: +1 AC 0.08 a round, +1 attack 0.1, +1 saves 0.03, temporary HP as a share of HP, quickened 0.3 a round. Attack and damage bonuses and quickened count only for the part of the target's threat its Strikes make, so a caster puts Haste on the fighter, not on itself. | Modifier rules are most of what PF2e's spell effects do. Situational ones would need the GM's call. |
| S19 | Spells live play leaves to PF2e's own spell card (kind `unsupported`: Telekinetic Projectile, Gouging Claw, Force Barrage, Lay on Hands). | Cast from the numbers the spell compiler reads from PF2e's own item data (`resolution.native`), so they roll what PF2e's card rolls at the table. Where the compiler found that the text disagrees with PF2e's data, the spell is listed as "simulated as PF2e rolls it". | PF2e's card is what happens at the table when the module stays out. |
| S21 | Area buffs (Bless: "you and allies in the area"). | The PF2e spell effect reaches the caster, the allies in its Melee, and (from a 10-foot emanation) the allies standing apart in its band. Foes caught in the area get nothing. | A support caster keeps close to its side. |
| S20 | A damage type the caster picks (Telekinetic Projectile, Murmuration). | The type the targets are weakest to, after their resistances and weaknesses. | What a sensible caster does. |
| S17 | Spells with action variants (Heal 1/2/3, Force Barrage). | The planner tries every variant it can afford as its own option and picks the best. Force Barrage puts all its missiles on one target. | Each variant is a real choice. Splitting missiles to finish two targets is a later refinement. |
| S22 | Scrolls, wands and staves (the user, 2026-10-14: "can we get the simulator to treat scrolls, wands and staffs as spells the creature can cast?"). | **The user chose every recommended answer:**<br>• **Who casts from them:** PF2e's own rule. A scroll, wand or spell gem at hand (not stowed, identified) is cast by the spellcasting that can cast its spell (a prepared or spontaneous entry of its tradition or with the spell on its list, or an entry for items), with that entry's DC and attack; PF2e picks the best DC. A PC with Trick Magic Item who has none rolls the skill check first (its best of the item's traditions' skills, DC by the item's level), an action more; then the spell DC and attack PF2e gives a trick (level as proficiency, the best of Int, Wis or Cha). A failure costs only the trick's action and the item for the turn; a critical failure, the item for the fight. The planner weighs the cast by the check's odds.<br>• **Staves:** PF2e Dailies' charges and spell list when Dailies has the staff prepared; otherwise the staff's description is read as Dailies reads it (its last list, a line per rank), its charges the creature's highest spell rank, one staff (the highest level). A spell costs its rank in charges; cantrips are free. A staff with a spellcasting entry of its own name (the iconics' "Staff of Fire") is that entry's, unless Dailies prepared it: then Dailies' charges replace that entry.<br>• **Scrolls:** like any limited spell, used whenever the planner thinks best; a stack casts once per scroll.<br>• **Stowed** (the user, 2026-10-14, on a stowed Scroll of Wails of the Damned listed as not simulated): taken out first, with PF2e's own cost to retrieve a stowed item (1 Interact; 2 from a backpack or a container meant to be held). A wand stays at hand after; each scroll is taken out for its cast. When the cast won't fit after it this turn (a 2-action spell from a backpack), the creature can take it out alone, worth part of the cast it readies, as a reload is.<br>• **Wands:** one cast a day (back after a rest). Once it's spent, the wand can be overcharged once, only while the fight goes badly for the caster's side (one of its side down or out, or its side behind on damage potential); the spell is cast, and the wand breaks (or is destroyed), which doesn't matter within one fight. The planner reaches for an overcharge last (its value ×0.95). | PF2e's own rules for consumables and Trick Magic Item; PF2e Dailies for staves when the table uses it. Not yet: a spontaneous caster's slot plus one charge (Dailies), and drawing a stowed scroll. |
| S23 | Who an area catches (2026-10-14, from the user's Wails of the Damned). | The creatures a spell can't affect (its target limits: "each living enemy", a level, a trait, a size) don't take the area's room and aren't listed among its targets. They were already left unaffected when it resolved. | The log names only who the spell can touch. |
| S24 | A condition value the text rolls ("Drained 1d4", Wails of the Damned). | Rolled when the condition is applied; the planner counts its average. Before, the value was lost and the condition applied at 1. | PF2e's text. |

**Spell effects** (S18): the core's `effect` family turns an `effect` plan into PF2e spell-effect changes, and the simulator reads the effect items' rules (`src/engine/effects.js`). The bestiary check compiles the way the spell module's official compile does (with its native-effect catalog). With that, unmodelled PF2e spell effects fell from 114 to 16.

**Healing and automatic damage** (step 3.2 in the core): the `healing` family (Soothe and similar: no check, recipients only) and the `direct-damage` family (Force Barrage's missiles by actions and rank, combined per target) are built. The plans arrive as the spell compiler writes their new payloads (`resolution.healing`, `resolution.byActions`, `resolution.missiles`).

**Conditions modelled** (from spells and from the actors): frightened, sickened, clumsy, enfeebled, drained, stupefied, slowed, stunned, quickened, off-guard, prone, fleeing, fatigued, paralyzed, asleep, blinded, dazzled, immobilized, restrained, grabbed, persistent damage, and death effects. **Not yet:** confused, controlled, fascinated, deafened and other text-only conditions. The gap list names each spell's missing parts ("spell, partly simulated").

**Measured** (`tools/bestiary-casters.mjs`, 2026-10-08): Monster Core 1–2 and NPC Core, 1,208 creatures, 501 of them casters with 5,038 spells.

- 2,392 of the spells can be cast in the simulation, and 1,453 of them are fully modelled (compiled the way live play compiles them, with the spell module's native-effect catalog and native numbers). Another 1,492 are utility spells.
- 1,503 fights ran with 3,293 casts and no errors.
- The biggest remaining gaps are note effects the GM plays (698), Heal and Harm's healing half (167), lingering areas (138) and PF2e spell effects (114).
- Of the other 3,308 spells, most are other resolution kinds the core doesn't resolve yet: effects, healing, summons and the like.
